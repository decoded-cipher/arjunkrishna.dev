I write code for a living, but I've never thought of programming as only my job. Every now and then I end up back in my editor after work, building something for myself — usually to find out whether I can.

<Margin>
  <span>2017 — Kristu Jyoti College</span>
  <span>2018 — Inovus Labs</span>
  <a href="https://blog.inovuslabs.org/nodrix-a-dream-that-refused-to-leave/">Read: Nodrix, a dream that refused to leave</a>
</Margin>

It started at Kristu Jyoti College, where I did my BCA and MCA. The college's innovation centre, [Inovus Labs](https://inovuslabs.org), had a small IoT workspace full of development boards, sensors and half-finished projects. I understood almost none of it, which is exactly why I kept going back. A few fried boards and popped capacitors later, I was hooked. I still mentor there.

<Margin>
  <span>2022 — Airtory</span>
  <span>2024 — Johnson & Johnson</span>
</Margin>

These days I'm a senior full-stack engineer at Johnson & Johnson, working on systems for product labelling. Before that I spent two and a half years at Airtory, building ad platforms for social media and connected TV.

<Margin>
  <a href="https://blog.inovuslabs.org/the-joy-of-learning-is-getting-lost/">Read: The joy of learning is getting lost</a>
</Margin>

The first website I made was a coming-soon page with a wallpaper of two horses and the Gilroy font. I don't remember a line of its code, but I remember how it felt to see it appear in the browser. I use AI every day and wouldn't give it up — but I miss the struggle it replaced, and that is what I write about most.
